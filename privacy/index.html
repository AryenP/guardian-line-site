Privacy policy

Guardian Line is a service for families. A family member (usually an adult child) sets it up for a parent. It answers some calls to the parent's phone with an automated assistant, screens them for scams and for callers that may be automated, and sends the family a short note. Nothing is installed on the parent's phone.

"We" means the people who run Guardian Line [legal name of the company, once incorporated].

Who the assistant answers

Calls the parent answers ring her phone as usual. We never hear them.

Setup changes a setting on the parent's phone so that calls she misses, declines or can't take are forwarded to a phone number we rent for her. Her phone rings as it does today, and calls she misses or declines go to the assistant. Whether that happens for a given call depends on her carrier and her phone, and we test it on each carrier before we say it works there.

When the assistant answers, its first sentence says it is an assistant. If a caller asks whether it is a person, it says it is not. It does not ask for or repeat card numbers, bank details, Social Security numbers, one-time codes or passwords.

The parent's agreement

The parent agrees to the service herself. Before the assistant answers her calls, she calls her assistant number from her own phone, hears what it does and presses 1 to agree. [This step is being built. Until it is live, the family member who sets up the service must have her agreement first, as the terms say.]

What we keep

We do not record calls and we store no call audio. While a call is live, the caller's speech is turned into text, and the assistant works from that text.

From each call the assistant answers, we keep a written transcript for a limited time, and a short call record for longer:

  • the time, the caller's number and the caller-ID check result from the phone network
  • what the caller said their name and reason were (short text)
  • a short summary and what happened (put through, message taken, call ended)
  • a scam score, and an AI score with its label, a confidence level and the reasons for it
  • which checks the assistant ran and any signs of a person it saw
  • timing measurements, such as how quickly the caller replied and how long our service took

We also keep what the family gives us: family members' names, phone numbers, email addresses and notification settings, the parent's name, phone number and carrier, the family's list of trusted and blocked numbers, facts about the parent that the family chooses to add (for example her bank or her pharmacy), and the family's safe word, stored only as a one-way hash that cannot be turned back into the word.

The facts about the parent are used only to check what a caller claims. They are never spoken on a call and never sent to the part of the service that speaks.

How we describe callers

An AI score is an estimate. We never tell a family a caller is certainly an AI. We show a label such as "Likely an AI caller" or "Not enough to tell", with a confidence level and the reasons.

Analysing caller audio in memory

Applies only when the feature is switched on. It is off today.

When it is on, the assistant also measures the sound of the caller's voice while the call is live (for example background noise, pauses and pitch) to help tell a person from a machine. The audio is held in memory only during the call. It is never written to disk, never stored, never sent to another company and is discarded when the call ends. We keep only the derived numbers, with the call record.

Who sees it

Everyone in the family's circle (up to five family members) sees the family's calls, notes, facts and settings. Only the family's owner can remove members, change the safe word or hand over ownership. A member who leaves or is removed loses access at once. A small team at Guardian Line can see call records to fix problems and to check calls a family marks as handled wrongly.

Who processes it

These companies process data for us to run the service:

  • Twilio: Carries the calls, rents the assistant’s phone numbers and runs the live speech connection.
  • Deepgram (through Twilio): Turns the caller’s speech into text, live.
  • ElevenLabs (through Twilio): Turns the assistant’s written lines into speech.
  • Anthropic: Reads the text of the call to score it and write the summary. It gets text, never audio.
  • Supabase: Database, sign-in and server functions (United States).
  • Fly.io: Runs the service that answers the calls (United States).
  • Resend: Sends email notes.
  • Expo, with Apple and Google: Delivers push notifications to family members’ phones.
  • GitHub: Hosts this website.

When paid plans start, Apple (App Store) and RevenueCat will process subscriptions bought in the app, and a payment provider not yet chosen will process web payments. We will list it here before anyone pays. This website does not use analytics or advertising trackers today.

Deleting your data

You can delete your account in the app's Settings. If you are the last member of your family, that deletes the family, its parents, facts, calls, transcripts and notes at once. If others remain, your own profile is deleted and the family stays with them. Deleting the account does not cancel an App Store subscription; cancel that in the App Store. To be removed from the early-access list, write to us.

What we never do

We do not sell your data or the data of anyone who calls. We do not share it for advertising.
