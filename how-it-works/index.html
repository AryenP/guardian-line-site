How it works

Her phone keeps ringing like it always has. She answers the calls she wants to. The calls she doesn't answer go to an assistant instead of her voicemail. It says what it is, asks who's calling, puts real people through to her and ends calls that are scams. You get a short note about every call.

1. Add your parent

Her name, number and carrier. The name is what the assistant says on the call, so use the one callers would know. Nothing gets installed on her phone.

2. Dial one code on her phone

We show you the exact code for her carrier. It takes ten seconds and it's reversible. Open the Phone app on her phone, dial the code exactly as shown and press call. You'll hear a confirmation tone or see a message.

On an iPhone, turn off Live Voicemail first: Settings → Phone → Live Voicemail.

To turn it off later, there is one more dial code. The app shows it for her carrier.

3. Call her to test

Let it ring. When the assistant answers, you're done. The app places the test call for you and shows "Mom's phone is protected" once the assistant picks up.

What it never does

  • It never asks for, or repeats, card numbers, Social Security numbers, bank details, one-time codes or passwords.
  • It never says it's a person.
  • It never records audio. It keeps a written transcript, and deletes it after 30 days.
  • It never answers the calls she picks up herself.

What you get

A short note about every call the assistant answers: who called, what they wanted and what happened. Scam calls are marked so you can mention them to her.
